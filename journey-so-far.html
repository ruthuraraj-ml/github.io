The Journey So Far

Every project in this portfolio came from coursework, a stall, or a wall I had to find my way around — not from a tutorial. This is the record of how that happened, in order.

Feb 2025 – Aug 2026 ~6 min read

I didn't set out to build an AI portfolio. I started learning Python to teach better — everything below followed from there, one course and one stall at a time.

Feb 2025
I registered for an NPTEL Python course
Not to become a programmer, but to teach better — including LeetCode problems for my own students. I cleared it on the first attempt. The struggle along the way made it obvious how shallow my actual understanding was.
So I went to Exercism to practice — which pulled me into LeetCode on its own terms, a habit with its own story I won't get into here. The following semester, I retook the NPTEL course. Silver, top 5%.
Jun 2025
My institution's AICTE QIP at IIIT Allahabad opened the door to AI
"From AI to GenAI" — my first real exposure to either. I had no formal grounding in ML or AI at all, so I stacked two more courses in the same semester just to keep up: AI: Concepts & Techniques and Introduction to Machine Learning.
The program demanded 6 projects and a capstone. The capstone became part of my PhD research, later submitted to a journal.
Nov 2025
QIP ended. I didn't stop.
An SRGAN project, built just after, pulled me further into generative AI — the first project that wasn't required by anything. The courses gave me theory. Building things from scratch — Word2Vec, a tokenizer, a ReAct agent with no framework at all — is how I checked whether I actually understood it.
Jan 2026
R-B.A.T begins — and immediately hits a wall
A Tutor workflow, grounded in prescribed textbooks, worked well. Partway through building the Assessment Generator that followed it, I ran into a hard token bottleneck on cloud APIs. Development didn't stall for lack of ideas — it stalled because the environment couldn't keep up with them.
Feb 2026
R-B.A.T goes quiet under two hard courses
Two more NPTEL courses that semester took most of my attention. The bottleneck stayed unsolved — not abandoned, just waiting.
Spring '26
A workshop mentions Ollama — R-B.A.T comes back
Near the end of my second course, local LLMs entered the picture almost by accident. Once inference wasn't metered anymore, the Assessment Generator finished — then Model Answer and Presentation Generation followed.
May–Jul 2026
Analytics Vidhya coursework
Analytics Vidhya gave me the frameworks — CrewAI, LangGraph, multi-agent architectures. The theory came earlier, from QIP and the NPTEL courses. The one concrete pattern I already had hands-on — deterministic logic paired with an LLM — came from R-B.A.T's stall and recovery. AV gave the rest a place to go.
Aug 2026
Here
Every project in this portfolio traces back to one of these stages.
None of this was a straight line. R-B.A.T stalled for a real reason and came back for an almost incidental one. Building things from scratch, over and over, is still how I check whether I've actually learned something. This is the order it happened in.
